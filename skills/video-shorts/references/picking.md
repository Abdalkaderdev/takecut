# Picking moments for shorts

Read the whole `transcript.txt` before choosing. Rank candidates, then take the best N the user
asked for (default 3). Prefer fewer strong clips over many weak ones.

## A good short

- **Length 20-60 s.** 25-45 s performs best for most talking content.
- **Hook in the first 3 seconds.** The opening line must make sense with zero context and create
  a question, tension or promise: a bold claim, a surprising number, a contrarian take, a story
  opening ("The worst bug I ever shipped..."), a direct "you" statement.
- **Self-contained.** No "as I said", "that", "this one", "he" referring to something outside the
  clip. Start on a sentence boundary, never on "And", "So", "But" unless the line still works.
- **One idea, with a payoff.** End on the punchline, the answer, the takeaway or a strong
  sentence end. Never end on "so..." or mid-list.
- **Energy.** Laughter, emphasis, short punchy sentences, concrete examples beat abstract
  explanation. Long monotone segments rarely work.

## Tricks

- Trim the throat-clearing: start at the strongest sentence even if the speaker warms up before it.
- Join two parts with `clips` when the setup and payoff are separated by a tangent. Keep the
  joined parts in original order unless the reorder still reads naturally.
- If the first line is weak but the clip is great, move a later hook sentence to the front with
  `clips` (hook first, then the full segment).
- Avoid segments with heavy on-screen references ("look at this chart") unless the visual is in frame.
